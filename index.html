My Story

A journey shaped by curiosity

I’m Rachel Nana Asiedua Yeboah , a Ghanaian biotechnology researcher with a passion for food science, sustainable agriculture, innovation, and creating opportunities for young people.

My journey has been shaped by curiosity, resilience, and a desire to understand how science can be used to solve real-world problems. That journey has taken me from Ghana to Italy, where I am completing my Master's degree in Biotechnologies for Food Science at the University of Padova.

This milestone represents far more than a degree. It represents learning, sacrifice, discovery, persistence, growth, and the people who encouraged me along the way.

Every classroom, experiment, challenge, friendship, and opportunity has become part of the story that brought me here.

Curiosity in Action

Into the Lab

Somewhere between the questions, experiments, unexpected results, and long laboratory days, curiosity became research.

Science with a purpose

I have always been interested in how science can improve the way we produce and protect our food. That interest has led me through plant science, food safety, molecular biology, and agricultural biotechnology.

“How can what we learn in the laboratory make life better beyond it?”

My Master's research focuses on the phytotoxicity and biostimulant potential of graphene-based materials in tomato seed priming.

I study how Graphene Acetic Acid and Nitrogen-Doped Graphene influence the early growth and development of tomato plants.

Behind every seedling, measurement, and dataset lies a larger question: could new approaches to seed treatment contribute to more sustainable and effective ways of producing food in the future?

Behind the Thesis

From experiment to evidence

Research has taught me that science is rarely a straight path.

It requires patience, attention to detail, good questions, careful work with data, and the willingness to learn from results that do not always turn out as expected.

Some of the most valuable lessons have come not only from experiments that worked, but also from the moments that required me to rethink, learn, and try again.

Every small step eventually became part of something much bigger: a thesis, a degree, and a deeper understanding of the kind of scientist I hope to become.

Another Side of Food Science

Protecting What We Eat

From production to protection

My journey in biotechnology has also given me practical experience in food safety and molecular biotechnology.

During my internship at the Istituto Zooprofilattico Sperimentale delle Venezie, I gained experience with techniques used for the molecular detection and characterization of foodborne pathogens, GMO detection, and food safety surveillance.

These experiences helped me appreciate food science from two important perspectives: how we produce food and how we ensure that the food reaching people is safe.

Producing enough food matters. Protecting the quality and safety of that food matters too.

More Than Science

Beyond the Lab

Learning, leading and serving

Science is an important part of my life, but it is not the only way I hope to make an impact. I am also passionate about young people, leadership, service, and creating opportunities for others.

As Head of Volunteering at YouthsMAP, I contribute to initiatives that support and empower marginalized youth in the United States and Sub-Saharan Africa.

This work has reminded me that making a difference does not always begin with something extraordinary. Sometimes it begins by sharing knowledge, creating an opportunity, offering encouragement, or helping someone recognize that they are capable of more than they imagined.

Science and youth development may seem like different worlds, but for me they are connected by the same purpose: using what I have learned to help create something better.

What Comes Next

The journey continues.

As I reach this milestone, I am proud of how far I have come, but I am equally excited about everything that still lies ahead.

I hope to build a career in research and innovation at the intersection of biotechnology, food science, and sustainable agriculture, contributing to work that addresses real challenges and has meaning beyond the laboratory.

I am especially interested in how science and technology can contribute to food systems that are safer, more sustainable, and more resilient.

And somewhere along the way, I hope my own journey can encourage another young person to remain curious, keep learning, and take opportunities when they come.

“I am still learning. Still growing. Still dreaming. And this is only the beginning.”
